# Critique: the owner (deputy security lead, decides what readers see on a paused day)

What I came to do, every time: a day paused, I have five minutes before a meeting. I need to know **how serious it is,
what readers see now and after each choice, and what is recommended and by whom**, then decide in **one tap, on
record**. Worst first. Before images are `S/peer/snap/<video>/NN.png`.

1. **The decision is buried under the evidence** (2-problem-fix/07 to 12, 828 to 883 words; 4b-outlier/06 to 10, 814 to
   836 words; 2-problem-fix/18, 1,009).
   - The left half opens with a chart's arithmetic ("Far past the ±5% limit: 20× the limit", "The limit pauses below
     2,199 rows or above 2,429; Mon 26 had 0") before I learn who recommends what.
   - The recommendation is an 11 px chip on the 2nd line of the first tile, wrapped over two lines, at the same volume
     as everything else.
   - Why it matters: in five minutes I read about 120 of 850 words. The one line I need is the one I find last.
   - Breaks §2 (words doing a picture's job, everything at the same volume), §3.5 (one focal point), §3.7 (the answer
     first, the method last).
2. **"One tap to decide" is four actions** (2-problem-fix/07 to 12; 4b-outlier/06 to 10).
   - Nothing comes chosen ("Choose one" is disabled), so I tap the tile Sloane recommended, then the small pill "Use
     Sloane Marsden's reason ›", then type my name, then the button.
   - Agreeing with her reason is a separate pill, easy to miss. Missing it puts "no reason given" on the record against
     a choice that had one.
   - Why it matters: every extra step is a place to stop, or to get the record wrong under time pressure.
   - Breaks §6 (one primary action) and §3.3 (primary, secondary and tertiary actions).
3. **On a phone, the recommendation and the choices start below the fold** (2-problem-fix/09 at 390x844, rendered from
   the snapshot).
   - The first screen shows the cause, the chart and the sticky footer. The recommended choice is cut off at its
     picture.
   - The panel then scrolls itself down to the question, which pushes the severity off the top.
   - Why it matters: a paused day reaches me on the phone, on the way to that meeting.
   - Breaks §4 (a phone shows one pane, the answer in it) and §6 (works at phone size).
4. **How serious it is doesn't read at a glance** (2-problem-fix/07, 4b-outlier/06).
   - The severity, "serious: no rows at all", is an 11 px pink chip. Around it are a pink title, a pink box, a pink bar
     and a pink banner, so nothing stands out.
   - The 28-day line chart is on a ±10% scale, so a 100% drop or a doubling sits off the chart as an arrow. The picture
     says "flat"; only the small bar pair says "none".
   - Breaks §3.6 (grey everything, then highlight one thing; bars from zero, a change on the same scale as the normal)
     and §2 (colour everywhere).
5. **What readers see after each choice takes about 120 words to compare** (2-problem-fix/07).
   - Four near-identical pictures, each with 9 px day labels and a 12 px eye. Only the eye's place and the second
     card's contents differ.
   - Each picture has three lines of pros and cons under it. "Done for now · reopens if a file comes" appears three
     times on one screen.
   - Breaks §2 (the same fact in three places), §6 (plain words, said once) and §3.6 (label directly).
6. **The same facts are said again and again on one panel** (2-problem-fix/11).
   - "11 outputs" appears four times.
   - The chosen consequence appears on the tile, again in the after-line ("You keep it paused, recorded: the 11 outputs
     keep waiting, and it stays on your list"), in "On record", and on the button.
   - Her quote appears on the tile and again, whole, in the record line.
   - Breaks §2 and §6 (said once).
7. **The page behind stays drawn while I decide** (2-problem-fix/07).
   - About 100 words of map labels, held chips and legend show through a 16% scrim. None of it bears on the choice.
   - Breaks §3.5 (one focal point).
8. **A corrected file's answer is the last four words of a 60-word paragraph** (4b-outlier/06).
   - "…status changed on 27 rows, 35 the day before. It passed every check", in a green box.
   - The answer I act on comes after the forensics.
   - Breaks §3.7 (the answer first, the evidence one tap away).

What works, and what I keep: the record (who, when, in whose words), the receipts after, the banner's Decide button, the
recommendation named with a person and a time, and the "readers see" idea itself.
